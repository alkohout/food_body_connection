Model Metrics Guide

Understanding Model Performance

This guide explains how to interpret the performance of the allergen ranking model. The model analyses your logged allergen and symptom data to highlight potential patterns. It is a tool for guidance, not a medical diagnosis.

Metrics and Interpretation

  • ROC AUC: Measures the model's ability to distinguish likely from unlikely allergens.
    ● ≥ 0.75: Good – strong separation
    ● 0.65 – 0.74: Moderate – some useful signal
    ● < 0.65: Weak – limited reliability
  • Symptom Recall: The proportion of symptom-linked allergens the model correctly identifies.
    ● ≥ 0.70: Good – most triggers captured
    ● 0.55 – 0.69: Moderate – some triggers may be missed
    ● < 0.55: Low – use results with caution
  • Sample Size: The number of logged allergen–symptom events used by the model.
    ● ≥ 200: Good – reliable patterns
    ● 75 – 199: Moderate – patterns emerging
    ● < 75: Low – preliminary, results may be unstable
  • Allergen Co-occurrence: Some allergens are often logged together. High co-occurrence may indicate that multiple allergens are consumed simultaneously, making it harder to isolate individual effects.
    ● No co-occurrence: Good – reliable odds ratios
    ● Co-occurrence present: Caution when interpreting odds ratios

Dose–Response Odds Ratio

The odds ratio (OR) and its 95% confidence interval are estimated using an Ordinal Logistic Regression Model, which determines how the probability of being in a higher symptom intensity category changes with allergen volume.

OR > 1: Higher allergen volume is associated with greater odds of more intense symptoms.
OR = 1: No association with symptom intensity.
OR < 1: Higher allergen volume is associated with lower odds of more intense symptoms.

The 95% confidence interval (CI) indicates precision:
If the CI does not include 1, the association is statistically significant.
If the CI includes 1, the association may be due to chance.

  • Odds Ratio: Quantifies how the odds of experiencing higher symptom intensity change with each unit increase in allergen volume.
    ● CI minimum > 1: Associated with greater odds of more intense symptoms
    ● CI maximum < 1: Associated with lower symptom intensity
